European citizens' initiative

Resident of the European Union? We invite you to sign the Good Food for All initiative (deadline 7 January 2027). It tackles the mainstream food system from many fronts, including to stop food waste, encourage fruit and vegetable consumption, and support peasant agroecological systems.

Citizen toolkit by École Jardin Forêt

What if your neighborhood could help feed its residents? A lawn, the space around an apartment building, a vacant lot, or a small public space can become much more than just a green area: it can become a small edible forest shared by an entire neighborhood. The mission of École Jardin Forêt is to share knowledge and give everyone the tools they need to create edible, living, and resilient landscapes. They have created a free citizen toolkit to help people move from idea to action.

Foraging in Pasadena by Ryen O'Meara

Hi, I'm Ryen. I grew up in Michigan but lived the last five years in Southern California, earning a PhD in Chemical Engineering by day at Caltech, attending culinary school by night. I love cooking and eating great food, and this region has it all: diverse cultures and cuisines, year-round farmers' markets (fresh tomatoes and berries in January?!), and an overwhelming abundance of publicly available fruit.

During my time in Pasadena, I harvested persimmons (Fuyu and Hachiya) for hoshigaki (干し柿), citrus (oranges, lemons, pomelos, kumquats) to eat fresh or as preserves, prickly pear (fruits and paddles, or nopales), loquats, herbs (rosemary, rose, lavender, sage, etc.) for drying and infusions, blue elderberry (blossoms and berries) for wine, figs, grapes, olives, passion fruits, and pomegranates (map).

After building a mental map of the city's fruit trees, I wondered whether a community tool existed to map them, such that when I inevitably move, I can continue foraging without starting over from scratch. Needless to say, I was very happy to find Falling Fruit and contribute to a community I feel strongly about: urban foraging establishes a connection to a place and publicly available food should be known and accessible, both to enhance food security and to minimize food waste. Just be sure to abide by the Honorable Harvest, asking permission (of the tree), taking only what you need, and reciprocating the gift you were just given.
